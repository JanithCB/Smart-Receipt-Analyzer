TITLE: Make a Budget
SOURCE: Federal Trade Commission
DOCUMENT_TYPE: worksheet
TOPICS: budgeting, monthly budget, income, expenses, spending categories, household expenses, transportation costs, health expenses, family expenses, finance fees

SUMMARY: This worksheet helps a user see how much money they spend in the current month and use that information to plan the next month’s budget. It organizes income and expenses into clear categories so a user can total monthly income, total monthly expenses, and compare the two. The worksheet also explains that expenses that do not occur every month should be placed in the “Other expenses this month” category. It ends with simple guidance for interpreting the result: if income is greater than expenses, money is left to save or spend, and if expenses are greater than income, the budget should be reviewed to find expenses to cut.

SECTION: Purpose of the budget worksheet
Use this worksheet to see how much money is spent this month. Then use this month’s information to help plan next month’s budget. Some bills are monthly and some come less often, so any expense that does not occur every month should be entered under “Other expenses this month.”

SECTION: How to use the worksheet

Enter the month and year for the budget period.

Record income for the month.

Record each expense under the matching category.

Add all income amounts to calculate total monthly income.

Add all expense amounts to calculate total monthly expenses.

Compare total monthly income with total monthly expenses.

If an expense does not occur every month, include it in “Other expenses this month.”

SECTION: Income categories

Paychecks: Salary after taxes, benefits, and check cashing fees.

Other income: Income after taxes, for example child support.

Total monthly income: The total of all income recorded for the month.

SECTION: Housing expenses

Rent or mortgage.

Renter's insurance or homeowner's insurance.

Utilities, such as electricity and gas.

Internet, cable, and phones.

Other housing expenses, such as property taxes.

SECTION: Food expenses

Groceries and household supplies.

Meals out.

Other food expenses.

SECTION: Transportation expenses

Public transportation and taxis.

Gas for car.

Parking and tolls.

Car maintenance, such as oil changes.

Car insurance.

Car loan.

Other transportation expenses.

SECTION: Health expenses

Medicine.

Health insurance.

Other health expenses, such as doctors' appointments and eyeglasses.

SECTION: Personal and family expenses

Child care.

Child support.

Money given or sent to family.

Clothing and shoes.

Laundry.

Donations.

Entertainment, such as movies and amusement parks.

Other personal or family expenses, such as beauty care.

SECTION: Finance fees and payment expenses

Fees for cashier's checks and money transfers.

Prepaid cards and phone cards.

Bank or credit card fees.

Other fees.

School costs, such as supplies, tuition, and student loans.

Other payments, such as credit cards and savings.

SECTION: Other expenses and totals
The worksheet includes a line for “Other expenses this month” for expenses that are irregular or do not occur every month. It also includes a line for total monthly expenses so the user can compare all spending against total monthly income.

SECTION: How to interpret the result
Maybe income is more than expenses, which means money is left to save or spend. Maybe expenses are more than income, which means the budget should be reviewed to find expenses to cut.

KEY_TERMS:

total monthly income: The total amount of income recorded for the month.

total monthly expenses: The total amount of all expenses recorded for the month.

other expenses this month: A category for expenses that do not occur every month.

housing expenses: Costs related to housing, utilities, insurance, communications, and other housing-related items.

food expenses: Costs related to groceries, meals out, and other food spending.

transportation expenses: Costs related to travel, vehicle use, maintenance, insurance, and loans.

health expenses: Costs related to medicine, health insurance, and other healthcare needs.

personal and family expenses: Costs related to dependents, family support, clothing, laundry, donations, entertainment, and personal care.

finance fees: Costs related to money transfers, prepaid products, and bank or credit card fees.

ACTIONABLE_GUIDANCE:

Use the worksheet to calculate how much money is spent this month.

Use this month’s spending information to plan next month’s budget.

Put any non-monthly expense in the “Other expenses this month” category.

Record salary after taxes, benefits, and check cashing fees under paychecks.

Record other income after taxes under other income.

Add income entries to get total monthly income.

Add expense entries to get total monthly expenses.

Compare income and expenses to see whether money is left to save or spend or whether expenses need to be cut.
