TITLE: Consumer tips for managing spending
SOURCE: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau
DOCUMENT_TYPE: worksheet
TOPICS: managing spending, budgeting, spending challenges, spending limits, tracking spending, financial well-being, household budget, balance monitoring

SUMMARY: This worksheet helps users identify common spending challenges and choose practical actions to better manage spending. It explains that improving financial well-being includes having more control over day-to-day and month-to-month finances, building a cushion for financial shocks, getting on track to meet financial goals, and having more freedom to make choices that allow enjoyment of life. The document guides users through identifying spending problems, creating a budget, setting spending limits, and monitoring progress. It ends with a simple personal action plan where users list specific steps they will take to manage spending.

SECTION: Purpose of the worksheet
This worksheet is designed to help users recognize common spending challenges and come up with ways to take action to manage spending. It connects spending management to financial well-being and presents a short process for identifying problems, choosing budgeting actions, and creating a personal plan.

SECTION: Financial well-being outcomes
Improving financial well-being means having more control over day-to-day and month-to-month finances, having a greater cushion to absorb a financial shock, getting on track to meet financial goals, and having more financial freedom to make choices that allow enjoyment of life.

SECTION: Step 1: Identify spending challenges
Users are instructed to review a list of common spending challenges and check the ones that apply to them. If one or more challenges apply, the worksheet directs users to try the spending-management tips that follow.

SECTION: Common spending challenges checklist

Not tracking or budgeting income and spending because it feels like a hassle.

Having tried to use a budget, but finding income and spending tracking too complicated or too time-consuming.

Struggling to pay bills and expenses because income is inadequate or irregular.

Overspending and assuming spending can be reduced later, but finding that hard to do.

Not thinking about money spent when using a debit card or credit card for purchases.

Treating oneself or others occasionally even when it is not affordable.

Avoiding thinking about the financial situation because it feels discouraging.

SECTION: Step 2: Address spending challenges
The worksheet provides four ways to address spending challenges: track spending, create a budget, decide how much to spend, and keep track of progress. These steps are meant to help users manage spending more consistently and align spending with financial goals.

SECTION: Track spending by category
Start by getting a handle on where money goes. Track spending for a week or a month to see how much is being spent in different categories. The worksheet does not provide fixed category names, so users should organize spending into the categories that best reflect their household spending patterns.

SECTION: Create a household budget
Create a household budget. Decide how much money can be set aside or saved for special occasions so life can be enjoyed without overspending. This step is presented as the foundation for setting later spending limits and comparing actual spending against planned spending.

SECTION: Decide how much to spend on small purchases
Set a weekly spending limit based on the budget for small purchases that can add up and keep a person from achieving aspirations. The worksheet suggests setting aside cash for these purchases at the beginning of the week and not spending more than that amount. It also instructs users to check balances before shopping and set a spending limit based on the budget.

SECTION: Keep track of budget progress
Compare actual spending to the budget monthly or more frequently. If spending is regularly out of line with the budget, make adjustments to the budget. The worksheet also recommends checking balances regularly on accounts, credit cards, and prepaid cards, and signing up for balance alert text message services from a financial institution. It notes that some balance-checking services may incur fees and users should check with their financial service provider.

SECTION: Budget categories to track in this worksheet

Income and spending: Track total money coming in and total money going out.

Spending categories: Track spending by category over a week or a month to see where money goes. The worksheet instructs users to use different categories but does not specify exact category labels.

Bills and expenses: Monitor required payments and recurring expenses, especially if income is inadequate or irregular.

Small purchases: Set a weekly spending limit for smaller purchases that can accumulate over time.

Special occasion savings: Decide how much to set aside or save for special occasions.

Account balances: Check balances on bank accounts, credit cards, and prepaid cards before spending and on a regular basis.

SECTION: How to use the worksheet

Check which spending challenges apply.

Track spending for a week or a month in spending categories.

Create a household budget.

Decide how much money to save for special occasions.

Set a weekly spending limit for small purchases.

Consider setting aside cash for small purchases at the start of the week and do not spend more than that amount.

Check balances before shopping and use the budget to set a spending limit.

Compare actual spending to the budget monthly or more often, and adjust the budget if needed.

Check account, credit card, and prepaid card balances regularly.

Sign up for balance alerts from a financial institution if available.

KEY_TERMS:

financial well-being: Having more control over finances, a greater cushion for shocks, progress toward financial goals, and more freedom to make life choices.
